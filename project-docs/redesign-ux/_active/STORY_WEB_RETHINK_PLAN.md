## 2. Data facts (live, 2026-09-25)
- **Country webs** (`SYSTEMS#<place>`): 16 places, including regions (Europe, Asia, Middle East). Each web holds the top ~15 stories of that place.
  - 89 distinct stories appear in any web; **25 appear in more than one** (e.g. Japan+US, Israel+Palestine+Middle East).
  - 64 causal links (model judgment) and 101 shared-actor links (fact).
  - 63 stories have ≥1 causal link; the maximum is 9.
- **Coverage is low:** of the 45 stories an anonymous reader sees in the last 14 days, **only 11 (24%) are in any web**.
  All webs are frozen since 09-10/12 (DeepSeek outage), and they expire from 2026-09-26 07:15 UTC (14-day TTL).
- **Links are already grounded in news:** every causal link cites the news items that evidence it (169 cited items in total).
  In the US web, **9 of 11 cited items match real entries** of the linked stories, with dates.
  - Nearly all sit on the *effect* side, e.g. link "US–Iran tensions → Trump's legal troubles" cites the Aug 18 item
    "Trump's approval rating falls to lowest of his presidency amid Iran war concerns".
  - So a link can be drawn from a story to a **specific dated news event** in another story.
- **No event-to-event links exist.** Nothing says which event inside the focal story drove the effect. Drawing that
  would be inference, unless a new pipeline generates it.
- **A story's own news:** `narrative_thread` returns its entries (headline, date, regions, sources with outlet and country), in 1.5–2 s,
  but up to ~530 KB for a big story. `inflectionTopicId` marks the turning point. `rootCauseChain` is prose (string or 3-part object).
- **World links** (`world_overview`) are country-to-country shared actors (e.g. Japan–US: Kim Jong-un, North Korea, Putin), not story links.

## 3. Questions to decide
1. **The unit.** What are the nodes and edges of a news-based web?
   - (a) story-centred: one story plus its neighbours, merged across every web it appears in;
   - (b) event-centred: the story's own news events as a spine, with links attached to the dated events that evidence them;
   - (c) a new per-story event graph generated by a new backend job.
2. **Honesty.** What can we draw truthfully from today's data, and what would be invented? In particular: event→event arrows,
   cross-country links, and "no web" for 76% of stories.
3. **Coverage.** What do the ~76% of stories without a web show? Is the story's own event spine enough to be the web?
4. **Frontend form** on the story page, and the knock-on for the home card, the board WEB view and the country page.
5. **Backend path.** Is a new generator needed now, later, or never? What would it cost, and does it depend on DeepSeek?
6. **Stage plan.** What ships first (frontend-only), and what waits for the backend or for DeepSeek?

## 4. Debate setup
- **Advocate A:** "Story-centred web from what exists", merging a story's neighbours across all its webs; frontend-only.
- **Advocate B:** "Event spine / metro map": the story's own events on a time spine; linked stories branch off at the cited events.
- **Advocate C:** "Build the real event web": a new per-story generator (events as nodes, causal links between news items,
  cross-country), with the frontend designed for it and an interim fallback.
- **Critic 1:** reader/analyst. **Critic 2:** engineering, data honesty and cost. Monitor rules (§5).
