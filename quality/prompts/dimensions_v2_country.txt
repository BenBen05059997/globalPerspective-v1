10. "dimensions": Object scoring this country's CURRENT risk across four INDEPENDENT axes. For each axis provide {"score": integer 0-100, "why": ONE sentence citing the specific arc/event from the data above that justifies the score} — or null when the data gives genuinely no signal for that axis. Be sparing: most countries are NOT elevated on all four — use null rather than a filler mid-number. A scored axis REQUIRES a cited fact in why; if the data does not discuss an axis at all, output null for it — never a guess and never 0 for "didn't come up." Score the PRESENT situation only: a future possibility ("could become a war," "could destabilize the government") is not a severity score — that belongs in riskSignals/trajectory, never in dimensions. Axes:
   - "conflict": armed violence, military operations, armed-actor intensity. War and war-risk belong ONLY here — do not also raise political for a country that is merely at war, sanctioned, or under diplomatic pressure. Band anchors: low = posturing or routine defense news, no active hostilities; moderate = localized skirmishes or a single clash with limited casualties; elevated = sustained fighting with meaningful casualties or a territorial/strategic effect; high = large-scale ongoing conflict, mass casualties, or direct state-on-state engagement.
   - "political": DOMESTIC institutional stability, governance, legitimacy, and protest/unrest ONLY. Being at war, sanctioned, diplomatically isolated, or criticized by a foreign leader is NOT political risk by itself — those are conflict or economic signals, or no signal at all. Score political only when the data shows an actual domestic legitimacy/governance signal. Band anchors: low = normal governance friction (a contested but orderly vote, a peaceful protest); moderate = real institutional strain short of a legitimacy crisis (a fracturing coalition, protests with some arrests); elevated = an actual legitimacy/governance crisis (mass protests met with force, an active impeachment, a disputed election with street mobilization); high = institutional collapse (a coup, a government falling amid unrest, martial law).
   - "economic": financial stress, sanctions, trade/market disruption. Band anchors: low = routine economic data or policy news with no disruption signal; moderate = a real but contained shock (a currency down several percent, a sector-specific tariff); elevated = broad-based financial stress or a major sanctions/trade action with cross-sector effect; high = a systemic crisis (sovereign default, a blockade choking a major export route, hyperinflation, a bank run).
   - "humanitarian": displacement, civilian harm, disaster, aid crisis. Band anchors: low = localized, contained hardship with adequate response capacity; moderate = a real but geographically/numerically contained event with aid access still functioning; elevated = large-scale displacement or aid access genuinely impaired; high = a mass-casualty or mass-displacement crisis with aid access severely restricted or a declared famine/atrocity.
Per-axis calibration: 0-24 = low, 25-49 = moderate, 50-74 = elevated, 75-100 = severe. (riskScore and riskLevel are derived from these — do NOT output them separately.)
